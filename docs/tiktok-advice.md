## Checklist for the new game

The advice from all 18 videos merged into one list, with repeats taken out. Tick items off as you plan and build. The last group isn't from the videos; I added it.

### First minutes

- [ ] A new player knows what to do within 10 seconds, without a tutorial
- [ ] They have a clear goal within the first 30 seconds
- [ ] The first small win comes within a few minutes, not after 20
- [ ] Features unlock gradually instead of all showing up in the first five minutes

### Progression

- [ ] Players can see upcoming rewards, upgrades and milestones before they unlock them
- [ ] There's always a next thing to work toward
- [ ] When players leave, they have unfinished business to come back for
- [ ] Every upgrade is actually useful, and the shop makes that obvious

### Feel

- [ ] No waiting around or dead time
- [ ] Every important action (buying, upgrading, finishing a task) gets a sound, an animation or visual feedback
- [ ] Players find their way through clues in the world instead of pop-up instructions

### Friends, shop and thumbnail

- [ ] There are reasons to play with friends: to interact, compete or team up
- [ ] The shop shows what each item is worth at a glance
- [ ] There are at least three moments worth clipping or talking about at school
- [ ] The thumbnail still stands out next to 20 competing games

### Real testing (my addition)

- [ ] Real kids play it before launch, and you watch where they get stuck or quit
- [ ] After launch, you check where players drop off in the Creator Dashboard

## Tip lists

### 5 Roblox Dev Tips (625f3401, 35s)

No. 1: Improve the first 30 seconds. Make sure players immediately understand what to do without needing a tutorial.

No. 2: Make progression more rewarding. Give players a clear reason to keep playing and working towards something.

No. 3: Remove unnecessary downtime. Cut anything that makes players wait around or feel bored.

No. 4: Make the game more social. Give players more reasons to interact, compete, or play with friends.

No. 5: Improve your game by making every important action feel more satisfying with better sounds, animations, or visual feedback.

### Keep players coming back (c5ae2c5b, 38s)

No. 1: Give them a goal immediately. Players should know what they're working towards within the first 10 seconds of playing your game.

No. 2: Show them what's coming next. Let players see future rewards, upgrades, or milestones before they unlock them.

No. 3: Create small wins. Players shouldn't have to play for 20 minutes before they feel like they've accomplished something.

No. 4: Introduce something new gradually. You don't have to reveal everything your game has to offer within the first five minutes.

No. 5: Give them unfinished business. When players leave, they should have a clear reason to want to come back to play your game.
